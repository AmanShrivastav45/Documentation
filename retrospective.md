

A retrospective isn't necessarily about identifying mistakes. It's about understanding what helped the team deliver and what slowed everyone down.

### 1\. What went well?

Think about these areas and see which ones genuinely reflect your sprint.

| Area                   | What you could talk about                                                                |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sprint delivery        | Did we complete our committed tickets? Were there unexpected challenges we handled well? |
| Team collaboration     | Did team members help each other resolve blockers?                                       |
| Communication          | Were updates, handovers, and discussions clear and timely?                               |
| Production stability   | Did our releases go smoothly? Were issues detected and resolved quickly?                 |
| Ownership              | Did people take responsibility for their tickets without needing frequent follow-ups?    |
| Technical improvements | Did we resolve technical debt, improve code quality, or simplify any process?            |

For example, if your recent release went smoothly, you could say:

> "One thing that went well was our release coordination. We managed to complete the planned activities, and the team was available to support whenever required."

### 2\. What could have been improved?

This is where I'd encourage you to focus a little more, because retrospective discussions are most valuable when they lead to actionable improvements.

| Area              | Questions to consider                                                           |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sprint planning   | Did we underestimate ticket complexity or commit to too much?                   |
| Dependencies      | Were tickets delayed because we were waiting for another team or clarification? |
| Testing           | Did we identify issues late in the sprint that could have been caught earlier?  |
| Code reviews      | Did merge requests remain pending longer than expected?                         |
| Knowledge sharing | Was work dependent on a particular person?                                      |
| Unplanned work    | Did production issues or urgent requests disrupt planned work?                  |
| Technical debt    | Did we repeatedly encounter problems caused by existing technical limitations?  |

For example:

> "One area we could improve is identifying dependencies earlier in the sprint. A few activities required clarification from other teams, and getting those answers earlier could help us avoid delays."

Notice how this focuses on improving the process rather than blaming someone.

### 3\. A few areas particularly relevant to your team

Based on the technical challenges you've discussed previously, I'd consider these three topics, provided they actually affected this sprint.

Renovate dependency updates: You've mentioned that dependency update MRs sometimes accumulate because your team works with sprint branches. You could discuss whether there's a better way to review and incorporate these changes regularly.

Technical debt visibility: Since you've already discussed creating a technical debt backlog with your manager, this could be a good opportunity to suggest making improvement opportunities visible and picking them up when capacity permits.

Release readiness: Given the importance of production stability for your application, you could discuss whether testing, deployment validation, and release coordination were handled effectively.

These are potential discussion areas, not necessarily problems you should raise today.

### 4\. A simple way to structure your feedback

I'd recommend using this structure:

1

Observation — What happened?

2

Impact — How did it affect the team?

3

Suggestion — What can we do differently?

For instance:

"Some of our tickets required additional clarification during the sprint. This caused a little delay in progressing them. Going forward, we could identify such dependencies during sprint planning itself."

It's concise, constructive, and gives the team something actionable.
